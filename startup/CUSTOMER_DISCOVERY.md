# Customer Discovery — Precast Company Interviews

*Last updated: 2026-02-06*

## Ground Rules (The Mom Test)

1. **Don't pitch.** You're here to learn, not sell. The moment you pitch, they start being polite instead of honest.
2. **Talk about THEIR life, not YOUR idea.** Bad: "Would you use AI to design precast?" Good: "Walk me through how a new panel gets designed."
3. **Ask about the past, not the future.** Bad: "Would you pay for this?" Good: "How much did you spend on design software last year?"
4. **Look for pain they're already spending money/time to solve.** If they're not already trying to fix it, it's not a real problem.
5. **Shut up and listen.** Aim for 80% them talking, 20% you.

---

## Interview Structure (~30-40 minutes)

### Opening (2 min)

"We're researching how precast companies handle their design and engineering workflows. I'd love to learn how things work at your company. There are no right or wrong answers — I just want to understand your day-to-day."

*Do NOT mention AI, Buildable, or your product.*

---

### Part 1: Understand Their World (10 min)

**Company basics:**
- How many people work at your company? How many are in the design/engineering team?
- What types of precast elements do you produce? (wall panels, beams, columns, double-tees, stairs, custom?)
- How many unique designs do you produce per month/year?
- What's a typical project look like? (number of elements, timeline from contract to delivery)

**The design process:**
- Walk me through what happens from when you get a new order to when the first element is produced.
- Who designs the elements? (in-house engineers, outsourced, the client's architect?)
- How do designs come in from clients? (architect's drawings, specs, just dimensions on paper?)
- What does the handoff from design to factory floor look like?

---

### Part 2: Current Tools (10 min)

**Software:**
- What software does your design team use today? (AutoCAD, Revit, Tekla, RFEM, hand drawings?)
- How did you choose that software? Who decided?
- How much do you pay for it per year? (licenses, maintenance, training)
- How many people know how to use it? What happens when that person is sick or leaves?

**Pain discovery (the critical questions):**
- What's the most time-consuming part of the design process?
- What's the most annoying part?
- Tell me about the last time a design error caused a problem in production.
- Have you ever lost a project or had delays because of the design phase?
- What would your team do with an extra 10 hours per week if design was faster?

---

### Part 3: Specific Workflows (10 min)

**Shop drawings:**
- How are shop drawings created? How long does a typical shop drawing take?
- How many revisions does a typical drawing go through?
- What causes most revisions? (dimension errors, rebar clashes, client changes, embed placement?)

**Rebar detailing:**
- How is rebar designed and detailed? (software, manually, outsourced?)
- How do you generate bar bending schedules?
- What's the biggest headache with rebar?

**Repetitive work:**
- How much of your design work is truly unique vs. variations of standard elements?
- Do you have templates or standard designs you reuse? How do you manage them?
- If a client says "same as last project but 200mm taller" — how long does that change take?

**Coordination:**
- How do you coordinate with the structural engineer? The architect? The erection crew?
- What file formats do you exchange? (DWG, IFC, PDF, paper?)
- What gets lost in translation between stakeholders?

---

### Part 4: Money & Decision-Making (5 min)

*These are the questions that determine if there's a business here.*

- How many hours per week does your design team spend on design work?
- What's the hourly cost of your design team? (salary + overhead, roughly)
- Have you looked for better tools in the last year? What did you find?
- If something could cut your design time in half, what would that be worth to you monthly?
- Who would make the decision to buy a new design tool? (you? the engineers? both?)
- What would need to be true for you to switch from your current setup?

---

### Closing (2 min)

- Is there anything about your design process I should have asked about but didn't?
- Would you be open to a follow-up conversation in a few weeks?
- Is there anyone else in the industry I should talk to? (other founders, engineers, factory managers)

---

## What You're Listening For

### Green Lights (real opportunity)

- [ ] "We spend X hours/week on repetitive design work" (quantified pain)
- [ ] "We lost a project because of design delays" (pain with consequences)
- [ ] "Our AutoCAD guy is retiring and nobody else knows it" (key-person dependency)
- [ ] "We tried [competitor] but it didn't work because..." (actively searching for solutions)
- [ ] "We pay $X,000/year for software that does half of what we need" (budget exists)
- [ ] "Most of our elements are variations of 5-10 standard types" (AI-automatable)
- [ ] They lean forward, ask to see something, or say "when can we try it?"

### Red Flags (might not be a real market)

- [ ] "Our process works fine" (no pain)
- [ ] "We use Excel and paper, and it's fine for our size" (not ready for software)
- [ ] "The architect gives us finished drawings, we just manufacture" (no design work)
- [ ] "We'd love it but could never pay for it" (no budget)
- [ ] "Our IT department/management would never approve new software" (buying friction)
- [ ] Generic enthusiasm without specifics — "sounds cool!" means nothing

## Target: 10 Conversations

Nikoloz has direct access through the Georgian Precast Association. Aim for:
- 5 conversations with precast company owners/founders
- 3 conversations with design engineers who do the actual work
- 2 conversations with factory/production managers (downstream customers of design)

The engineers will tell you what's painful. The owners will tell you if they'll pay. The factory managers will tell you where design errors actually hurt.

After 10 conversations, you'll know:
1. Is this a real problem worth solving?
2. What specific workflow to build first?
3. How much they'd pay?
4. How to position it?

If the answer to #1 is no — you saved months of building the wrong thing.
